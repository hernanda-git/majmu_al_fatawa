Chapter on Zihār

Shaykh al-Islām Ahmad ibn Taymiyyah—may Allah sanctify his soul—was asked about a man who said to his wife, “You are to me like my mother and my sister.”

He answered:

If he meant, “You are to me like my mother and my sister in the respect I accord you,” then nothing is required of him. But if he intended to liken her to his mother and sister with regard to “marital relations,” this constitutes zihār, and he is subject to its requirements. If he keeps her as his wife, he must not have intercourse with her until he has offered the prescribed expiation for zihār.

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was also asked about a man who married and wanted to consummate the marriage on a particular night, saying, “Otherwise, she will be to me like my mother and my sister.” She was not ready for him at the time he had requested her. Does a divorce take effect?

He answered:

No divorce takes effect according to any of the four schools of law. However, he has pronounced zihār. If he wishes to consummate the marriage, he must first offer the expiation Allah mentioned in Surah al-Mujādalah: freeing a believing slave; if he cannot find one, fasting for two consecutive months; and if he is unable to do so, feeding sixty needy people.

He—may Allah Most High have mercy on him—was asked about a man who became angry with his wife and said, “If I have intercourse with you again, it is like having intercourse with my mother beneath the coverings of the Kaaba.” Is he permitted to reconcile with her?

He answered:

All praise belongs to Allah. If he has intercourse with her, he is liable for the expiation for zihār: freeing a believing slave; if he cannot find one, fasting for two consecutive months; and if he is unable to do so, feeding sixty needy people. He must not have intercourse with her until he has offered the expiation.

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked about two men, one of whom said to the other, “My brother, do not do these things in your wife’s presence; it is unseemly of you.” The other replied, “She is just like my mother.”

His companion said, “Why did you say that? I have heard that a wife becomes forbidden to her husband through these words.” The husband then repeated his statement, saying, “Yes, by Allah, she is like my mother to me.” Does she become forbidden to her husband through these words?

---

He answered:

All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds.

If, by saying, “She is like my mother,” he means that she conceals his faults and neither disgraces nor reproaches him, just as a mother treats her child, then he is to be disciplined for this statement, but his wife does not become forbidden to him. For ʿUmar ibn al-Khattāb—may Allah be pleased with him—heard a man address his wife as “My sister,” so he disciplined him—though if he was unaware, he would not be disciplined for this, even if he deserved punishment for the wrongdoing he had committed—and said, “Is she your sister?” A man should therefore not liken his wife to his mother.

If, however, by “She is like my mother to me,” he means refraining from intercourse, sexual enjoyment, and other acts forbidden with one’s mother—as though saying, “She is like my mother, with whom sexual enjoyment is impermissible”—then he is “one who has pronounced zihār” and is subject to the obligations this entails. He may not have intercourse with her until he offers the “expiation for zihār”: freeing a slave; if he cannot find one, fasting for two consecutive months; and if he is unable to do so, feeding sixty needy people. Once he has done this, intercourse becomes lawful for him by the consensus of the Muslims—unless he intends, “She is forbidden to me like my mother.” In that case, he is regarded as having pronounced zihār according to the schools of Abū Hanīfah, al-Shāfiʿī, and Ahmad.

A disagreement has been reported within Mālik’s school as to whether a threefold divorce takes effect through these words. The unquestionably correct view is that no divorce takes effect thereby. By their unanimous agreement, intercourse is not lawful for him until he offers the expiation, and no divorce takes effect through these words. Allah knows best.

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked:

About a man who said to his former wife, whom he had irrevocably divorced, “If I take you back, you will be like my mother and sister”: may he take her back, and what is required of him?

He answered:

According to one of the two scholarly views, he must offer the expiation for zihār if he takes her back; according to the other, nothing is required of him. The first view is more cautious.

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked:

About a man who, in anger, said to his wife, “You are as forbidden to me as my mother.”

He answered:

This man has pronounced zihār against his wife and falls under Allah’s words:

“Those of you who declare their wives as unlawful to them as their mothers—their wives are not their mothers. Their mothers are only those who gave birth to them. They certainly utter an objectionable statement and a falsehood. Yet Allah is indeed Pardoning, Forgiving.”

“Those who declare their wives as unlawful to them as their mothers, then go back on what they have said, must free a slave before the two touch one another. This is what you are admonished to do, and Allah is All-Aware of what you do.”

“But whoever cannot find a slave must fast for two consecutive months before the two touch one another. Whoever is unable to do so must feed sixty needy people.”

Thus, if this man wishes to keep his wife and have intercourse with her, he must not approach her until he has offered the expiation Allah has specified.

Chapter on the Establishment of Lineage

He—may Allah Most High have mercy on him—was asked:

About a man who married a virgin who had reached maturity, consummated the marriage, and found her to be a virgin. She then gave birth to a child six months after the consummation. Is the child attributed to him or not? If the husband swore on pain of divorce that the child was his own biological son, does divorce take effect or not? The child is a healthy, fully formed boy who has lived for several years. Please give us a ruling; may you be rewarded.

He—may Allah be pleased with him—answered:

Praise be to Allah. If she gave birth more than six months after he consummated the marriage with her, even by a moment, the child is attributed to him by the agreement of the imams. A similar case occurred during the time of ʿUmar ibn al-Khattāb—may Allah be pleased with him.

The Companions established the possibility of a child being born after six months’ gestation by citing Allah Most High’s words:

“His gestation and weaning take thirty months,”

together with His words:

“Mothers shall breastfeed their children for two full years.”

If breastfeeding accounts for two years out of those thirty months, gestation is six months. Thus, the verse combines the minimum period of gestation with the full period of breastfeeding.

The child is attributed to him even if he has not acknowledged him as his own; how much more so when he has claimed and acknowledged him! Indeed, even if he acknowledged someone of unknown lineage, saying, “He is my son,” that person would be attributed to him by the consensus of the Muslims, provided that this was possible and no one else claimed him as his son. He has therefore been truthful in his oath and has not broken it. Allah knows best.

He—may Allah Most High have mercy on him—was asked:

About a man who purchased a virgin slave woman and had intercourse with her. She does not leave the house, and she is pregnant by him. He took her to the market, denying that the child was his and swearing to that effect.

He answered:

If he admits having had intercourse with her—for example, if he has previously acknowledged it—the child is attributed to him, and the pregnancy is deemed to be from him, provided that she gives birth within a period that makes his paternity possible. He may sell neither the unborn child nor its mother. However, if he claims to have ascertained that she was not pregnant through the prescribed waiting period, the scholars disagree over accepting his statement and requiring him to swear an oath. Allah knows best.

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked:

About a man who married a woman who remained with him for fifteen days, after which he divorced her irrevocably. She subsequently married another man after declaring that her waiting period following the first marriage had ended. The second husband then divorced her after six years. She brought forward a daughter, claiming that she was the first husband’s child.

Is her claim valid and binding upon the first husband, even though it has not been established that she gave birth to the girl? This husband and the woman live in the same town, and nothing prevented her from asserting the claims women make, or from demanding maintenance or a fixed allowance from him.

He answered:

Praise be to Allah. In these circumstances, this child—the girl—cannot be attributed to him merely on the basis of her claim, according to the unanimous agreement of the imams. Indeed, even if a divorced woman claims to have given birth in circumstances in which the child, if born to her, would be attributed to him, and he denies that she gave birth, her claim to have given birth is not accepted unless she produces evidence establishing it. There is no disagreement on this.

The testimony of one woman suffices according to Abū Hanīfah and the well-known position reported from Ahmad. According to Mālik and another position reported from Ahmad, two women are required. Al-Shāfiʿī requires four women. It suffices for the husband to swear that he does not know that she gave birth to the child.

If, however, the marriage is still in effect, there are two positions within Ahmad’s school. The first is that her statement is not accepted, as in al-Shāfiʿī’s school. The second is that it is accepted, as in Mālik’s school.

If her waiting period has ended and the maximum gestation period has elapsed, and she then claims to be pregnant by the first husband who divorced her, her statement is not accepted, without disagreement. Indeed, if she declares that her waiting period has ended and then gives birth six months or more later, but within the maximum gestation period, is the child attributed to him? The scholars have two well-known positions on this. The position of Abū Hanīfah and Ahmad is that the child is attributed to him. This is also the view adopted by Ibn Surayj, one of al-Shāfiʿī’s followers. However, the well-known position in the schools of al-Shāfiʿī and Mālik is that the child is not attributed to him.

This disagreement applies when she has not remarried. If, however, she marries after declaring that her waiting period has ended, and then gives birth more than six months after that marriage, the child is not attributed to the first husband, without disagreement. Once you understand the imams’ positions on these two principles, how, then, can the child be attributed to him

---

## Page 16216

merely on the basis of her claim after six years? Even if she said, “I gave birth to her at that time, before he divorced me,” her statement would not be accepted either. Rather, his statement is accepted, supported by his oath that she did not give birth to the girl while married to him. Likewise, if she said, “I delivered this child before marrying the second husband,” and the first husband denied this, his statement that she did not give birth to her before marrying the second husband is accepted. This is especially so given that she delayed making her claim until she had married the second husband, for this indicates that her claim is false—particularly according to Mālik’s principle concerning claims delayed without excuse when they could have been made earlier, in these and similar matters.

He—may Allah Most High have mercy on him—was asked about a man who pronounced three divorces against his wife. A mufti advised him that the divorce had not taken effect, so the husband followed his ruling and subsequently had intercourse with his wife, who bore him a child. It was then claimed that the child was born of fornication.

He answered:

Anyone who says that is guilty of the utmost ignorance, misguidance, and defiance of Allah and His Messenger. The Muslims agree that whenever a husband has intercourse within a marriage he believes to be permissible, the child born to him within it is attributed to him, and father and child inherit from one another by unanimous agreement of the Muslims—even if that marriage is, in fact, invalid by unanimous agreement of the Muslims. This applies whether the husband is a disbeliever or a Muslim. If a Jewish man marries his brother’s daughter, the child born of that union is attributed to him and inherits from him by unanimous agreement of the Muslims, even though that marriage is invalid by unanimous agreement of the Muslims.

---

## Page 16217

and anyone who regards it as lawful is a disbeliever who must be called upon to repent. Likewise, if an ignorant Muslim marries a woman during her waiting period, as ignorant Bedouins do, and has intercourse with her believing her to be his wife, the child born to him through her is attributed to him and inherits from him, by unanimous agreement of the Muslims. There are many such examples. “Establishing paternity” does not require the marriage to be valid in reality. Rather, the child belongs to the marital bed, as the Prophet—may Allah bless him and grant him peace—said:

“The child belongs to the marital bed, and the fornicator has nothing.”

Thus, if a man pronounces three divorces against his wife and has intercourse with her believing that the divorce has not taken effect—whether through ignorance, because he followed an erroneous ruling given by a mufti, or for some other reason—the child is attributed to him, and father and child inherit from one another, by unanimous agreement. Indeed, her waiting period is not reckoned until he ceases to have intercourse with her. Since he was having intercourse with her believing her to be his wife, she remained his partner in bed; her waiting period from him therefore does not begin until she ceases to share his bed.

If a man marries a woman in an “invalid marriage,” whether its invalidity is agreed upon or disputed; acquires ownership of her under an invalid title, whether its invalidity is agreed upon or disputed; or has intercourse with her believing her to be his free wife or a slave woman whom he owns, then the child born to him through her is attributed to him, and father and child inherit from one another, by unanimous agreement of the Muslims. The child is also free, even if the woman with whom he had intercourse actually belongs to someone else and the intercourse took place without her master’s permission. Yet, since the man was deceived concerning her—being married to her and told, “She is free,” or purchasing her when she was sold, believing that she belonged to the seller—he only had intercourse with a woman whom

he believed to be his free wife or a slave woman whom he owned. His child by her is therefore free on account of his belief, even if that belief was mistaken. This was the ruling of the Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the leading scholars of the Muslims agreed upon it.

As for these men who had intercourse and fathered children, even if they had done so within marriages unanimously held to be invalid, and their divorces had taken effect by unanimous agreement of the Muslims, yet they had intercourse believing that their marriages remained in force—because of a ruling someone had given them or for some other reason—the children would be attributed to them and would not be children of fornication. Rather, fathers and children would inherit from one another, by unanimous agreement of the Muslims. If this applies where the marriage is unanimously held to be invalid, how much more so where its invalidity is disputed?

This holds even when the opinion on which a man relied in having intercourse was weak, as in the case of a temporary marriage or a marriage contracted by a woman on her own behalf without a guardian or witnesses. If he has intercourse within such a union believing it to be a marriage, the child is attributed to him. How much more so, then, in a disputed marriage whose validity has been clearly supported by the Quran, the Sunnah, and legal analogy, when thorough examination has revealed the weakness of the opposing view and its proponents’ inability to defend it, for lack of any proof recognized by Islamic law?

Whoever says that a child born of this marriage, or one like it, is a child of fornication, and that the child and the father who had intercourse cannot inherit from one another, contradicts the consensus of the Muslims and has forfeited his standing in religion. If he is ignorant, he must be instructed and have it explained to him that the Messenger of Allah—may Allah bless him and grant him peace—his Rightly Guided Caliphs, and all the other leading scholars of the religion attributed the children of the people of the pre-Islamic era to their fathers, even though their marriages were forbidden by unanimous agreement. They did not make attribution of paternity conditional upon the marriage being permissible under Islamic law. If he persists in opposing the Messenger after

It has thus become clear that whoever rejects the fatwa that the divorce does not take effect, claims consensus that it does take effect, and declares the child to be a child of fornication is himself contradicting the consensus of the Muslims, the Book of Allah, and the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah, the Lord of all worlds. The judge said, “You must swear that she is not her daughter.” The man refused to swear anything other than, “She is not my daughter.” Someone accompanying him said to the judge, “It is not lawful for this man to swear that she is not this woman’s daughter.” The judge struck him with a rod and treated him harshly. The man then swore the oath, and the judge recorded an order imposing financial provision for the girl upon him. Is this order valid?

He replied:

All praise belongs to Allah. He must swear that she did not give birth to the girl during her waiting period, or while married to him, or in his home, under circumstances in which the child’s paternity could be attributed to him. He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked:

A man married a woman but neither consummated the marriage nor had intercourse with her. She gave birth two months later. Is the marriage valid, and is he liable for the dower?

He replied:

All praise belongs to Allah. By agreement of the Muslims, the child’s paternity is not attributed to him. Likewise, by agreement of the Muslims, he does not become liable for the full dower. However, scholars hold “two opinions” concerning the marriage contract.

The more correct opinion is that the contract is invalid, as held by Mālik, Ahmad, and others. In that case, the couple must be separated, and he owes neither a dower, nor half a dower, nor a parting gift, just as with other invalid marriage contracts when separation occurs before consummation. However, the separation should be ordered by a judge who considers the contract invalid, so as to settle the dispute.

The “second opinion” is that the contract is valid, but he may not have intercourse with her until she gives birth, as held by Abū Hanīfah.

It has also been said that he may have intercourse with her before she gives birth, as held by al-Shāfi‘ī,

Chapter on Waiting Periods

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked:

A woman was divorced by her husband on the twenty-eighth of Rabīʿ al-Awwal and then menstruated once. She subsequently married on the twenty-third of Jumādā al-Ākhir of the same year, claiming that she had menstruated three times, although she had menstruated only once. When the second husband learned of this, he too divorced her, issuing a single pronouncement during the first ten days of Shaʿbān of that year. She then wished to remarry the second man who had divorced her, claiming that she had reached menopause. Is her claim to be accepted, and may she be married?

He replied:

Menopause cannot be established by a woman’s assertion. However, if this woman says that her menstruation has ceased and she does not know why, she must wait a year. If she does not menstruate during that year, she may be married. If she has reached menopausal age, no such delay is required. If her menstruation is known to have ceased because of illness or breastfeeding, she remains in her waiting period until that condition ends.

This woman was required to observe “two waiting periods”: one for the first husband and another on account of intercourse with the second. Her marriage to the second man is invalid and requires no divorce to end it. Thus, if she menstruated only once and the cessation of bleeding continued,

---

## Page 16223

she must observe both waiting periods in months: six months after separating from the second man, if she has reached menopause. If the reason for the cessation of her menstruation is uncertain, the period is one year and three months.

This follows the view of those who hold that the two waiting periods do not overlap, such as Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī and Ahmad. According to Abū Hanīfa, waiting periods arising from relations with two different men overlap; however, he defines menopause by age. The view we have mentioned is the better and easier of the jurists’ two opinions, and it accords with the rulings of ʿUmar and others. According to the other opinion, a woman whose menstruation has ceased for an unknown reason remains in her waiting period until she reaches menopausal age. On their view, she would remain unable to marry until she reaches fifty or sixty years of age. This, however, entails hardship and undue difficulty in religion and undermines the interests of the Muslims.

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked:

A man married a woman who has now been with him for four years without menstruating. She stated that she had also not menstruated for four years before their marriage. Her husband then pronounced three divorces upon her. How may she marry another husband, and what waiting period must she observe, given that she is fifty years old?

He replied:

Praise be to Allah. According to the stronger of the scholars’ two opinions, she must observe the waiting period prescribed for women who have reached menopause: “three months.” She knows that her menstruation has ceased, and she knows that its cessation has been continuous. This is unlike a woman whose situation is uncertain and who does not know why her menstruation has ceased: whether it is a cessation

---

## Page 16224

due to menopause, or to a temporary cause, such as illness or breastfeeding, after which menstruation returns? These, then, are “three categories.”

When menstruation ceases because of a temporary cause, such as illness or breastfeeding, she must undoubtedly wait until that cause has passed. When it ceases and she does not know why, the position of Mālik and Ahmad—as explicitly reported from him—and one opinion of al-Shāfiʿī is that she observes the waiting period prescribed for menopausal women after first waiting out the duration of pregnancy. This accords with the ruling of ʿUmar. The position of Abū Hanīfa and al-Shāfiʿī’s later opinion is that she must wait until she reaches menopausal age and then observe the waiting period prescribed for menopausal women. This causes her great harm, for she may remain unable to marry for twenty, thirty or forty years. Such hardship has been lifted from this community. Women who have reached menopause, however, observe a waiting period of three months, according to the explicit text of the Quran and the consensus of the Muslim community.

The scholars disagree, however: is there a fixed age for menopause, after which any bleeding can only be postmenopausal bleeding? Is that age fifty or sixty, or does the matter require further distinctions? They also disagree over whether menopause can be established without reference to age.

This woman has reached menopausal age according to one of the two opinions, namely fifty, and she has not menstruated for a long time. She has also stated that she took something to stop the bleeding, and bleeding can be induced by medication. She therefore has no expectation that her menstruation will return. Thus, she is among those who have reached menopause and must observe their prescribed waiting period. And Allah knows best.

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was also asked:

A judge annulled a woman’s marriage shortly after she gave birth, having established that she had suffered harm because her husband had ceased providing maintenance and had failed to act toward her as prescribed by Islamic law, for a period sufficient to warrant annulment in her circumstances. Three months after the annulment, someone wished to marry her. May she calculate her waiting period in months, since most women do not menstruate while breastfeeding, or must her hardship continue until breastfeeding ends and her menstruation returns?

He replied:

Praise be to Allah. Rather, she must remain in her waiting period until she has had three menstrual periods, even if this is delayed until the breastfeeding period ends. This is agreed upon by the four imams and others. ‘Uthmān ibn ‘Affān and ‘Alī ibn Abī Tālib ruled accordingly among the Emigrants and the Helpers, and no one disagreed with them.

If the woman wishes to engage a wet nurse for her son so that her menstruation may return, or to drink something that will induce menstruation, she may do so. And Allah knows best.

If she belongs to “the first category,” her marriage is invalid. The judge who separated them was correct to do so and was also correct to discipline whoever did this. If she belongs to “the second category” and a judge married her, no other judge was entitled to separate them, and no divorce took effect, for a judge may act in such a matter according to the sounder of the two views.

He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked:

A woman whose hair had turned grey, but who had not reached menopause, used to menstruate regularly. She took medicine, whereupon her bleeding stopped and remained absent. Her husband then divorced her while she was in this condition. Is her waiting period to be calculated in months from the time of divorce, or must she wait until she reaches menopause?

He replied:

All praise is due to Allah, Lord of all worlds. If she knows that her bleeding will [not] (1) return, her waiting period is three months. If it may or may not return, she should wait until a year has passed and then marry, as ‘Umar ibn al-Khattāb ruled concerning a woman whose menstruation ceases without her knowing what caused it: she should wait a year. This is the position of the majority, including Mālik and al-Shāfi‘ī.

As for the view that she must reach menopause, it is very weak indeed, quite apart from the harm it entails—harm of a kind the Sharia does not prescribe. Or should she be prevented from marrying when she needs to, only to be permitted to marry when she no longer needs to?

A man suffered an illness that continued until his death. He—may Allah have mercy on him—was asked:

A man married a woman three years ago and was blessed with a child by her, now two years old. She stated that when she married him, she had experienced only two menstrual periods, and her husband accepted her account. He had divorced her a second time under this same marriage contract. Does divorce take effect under this annulled contract?

He replied:

If the husband accepts her statement that she married before her third menstrual period, the marriage is invalid, and he must separate from her. ‘Here you are, arguing on their behalf in this worldly life—but who will argue with Allah on their behalf on the Day of Resurrection, or who will be their defender?’

And so on to the end of the verses. The occasion for this was that a group called Banū Ubayriq stole

food and two coats of mail belonging to some of the Ansār. The owner of the property came to complain to the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace. The Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, did not tell the owner of the property, “Produce evidence,” nor did he have the accused swear oaths, because those accused were known for wrongdoing and there were evident grounds for suspecting them.

Likewise, the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, ruled by qasāma—the procedure of sworn oaths in homicide cases—when there were supporting indications making the claimants’ truthfulness probable. These matters concern penal sanctions serving the public interest, not private rights. Without qasāma in homicide cases, bloodshed would ensue: a man could kill his enemy in secret, while the victim’s next of kin would be unable to produce evidence. Swearing an oath is easy for a murderer, thief, or highway robber, for someone who regards such deeds as permissible will think little of an oath.

As for the Prophet’s saying, may Allah bless him and grant him peace:

“Were people granted their claims merely on their allegations, some would claim the lives and property of others. Rather, the oath rests upon the defendant.”

This applies where the claimant can offer no proof beyond the allegation itself. Nothing is awarded on that basis; instead, the defendant swears an oath.

If, however, the claimant produces one witness concerning property, the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, ruled in property cases on the basis of one witness and an oath. This is the position of the jurists of the Hijāz and the scholars of hadith, such as Mālik, al-Shāfi‘ī, Ahmad, and others. When there were supporting indications in a homicide claim, the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, said to the claimants:

“Will you swear fifty oaths and thereby establish your claim concerning your companion’s blood?”

The same applies to “highway robbers” and “thieves.” This concerns the public interest, not private rights. People cannot be secure in their lives and property, in their homes or on the roads, unless such offenders are deterred by amputation. Requiring each of them to swear an oath does not deter them.

For this reason, the jurists agree that a highway robber acting to seize property must be executed, and that his execution is a prescribed punishment owed to Allah, not a matter left to the discretion of the slain person’s next of kin. They explain that he did not kill the victim because of any personal grievance against him; rather, he killed him for his property. To him, there is no difference between this victim and anyone else. Executing him therefore serves the public interest, and the ruler must enforce it.

Likewise, a “thief” does not seek one particular person’s property; rather, he seeks to take this person’s property and that person’s property. Thus, amputating his hand is a binding right owed to Allah, not to the property owner. The owner recovers his property, and the thief’s hand is amputated. Even if the owner says, “I give him my property,” the punishment of amputation is not waived. Thus, Safwān said to the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace:

“I give him my cloak.”

The Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, replied:

“Why did you not do that before bringing him?”

The Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, also said:

“Whoever intercedes to prevent one of Allah’s prescribed punishments has opposed Allah in His command. Whoever knowingly argues a false case remains under Allah’s displeasure until he desists. Whoever says something about a Muslim that is not true of him will be confined in Radghat al-Khabāl—the mire of filth—until he frees himself from what he has said.”

He also said to al-Zubayr ibn al-‘Awwām:

“Once cases involving prescribed punishments reach the ruler, may Allah curse the intercessor and the one whose intercession is accepted.”

A similar case is that of someone shown to possess property which he is obliged to produce: for example, a debtor who is found to have concealed his wealth and insists on remaining imprisoned, or someone who holds property in trust but fails to return it to its rightful owner, and whose dishonesty has become evident. Such a person is not asked to swear an oath; rather, he is beaten until he produces the property he is obliged to produce or discloses its whereabouts. This is illustrated by the following account:

In the year of Khaybar, the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, spoke to al-Zubayr ibn al-‘Awwām concerning the paternal uncle of Huyayy ibn Akhtab. The Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, had concluded a peace agreement with them stipulating that the gold and silver would belong to him. He asked this man:

“Where is Huyayy ibn Akhtab’s treasure?”

He replied:

“O Muhammad, expenses and wars have consumed it.”

He said:

“There was a great deal of wealth, and too little time has elapsed for that.”

Then he said to al-Zubayr:

“Take charge of this man.”

Al-Zubayr subjected him to some torture, whereupon he directed them to the treasure in a ruined building nearby.

When this man said, “Expenses and wars have consumed it,” ordinary experience contradicted his claim, so no heed was paid to it. Rather, the Prophet ordered that he be punished until he directed them to the property. The same ruling therefore applies to anyone who takes other people’s property and claims that it has been lost in circumstances where ordinary experience contradicts his claim.

He—may Allah Most High have mercy on him—was also asked:

A man had gold sewn into his garment and, forgetting about it, gave the garment to a washerman. When the washerman returned it after washing it, he found that the place where the gold had been sewn in had been unstitched, and the gold was missing. What is the ruling concerning this?

Answer:

Either the defendant swears an oath clearing himself of liability, or the claimant swears that the defendant took the gold unlawfully, in which case the defendant must compensate him for it. If the washerman is known for wicked conduct, and the opened seam gives evident grounds for suspicion, it is permissible to beat him and subject him to discretionary punishment. Allah knows best.

Chapter: The Prescribed Punishment for Highway Robbers

Shaykh al-Islām—may Allah sanctify his soul—was asked:

Some people waylay Muslims, kill anyone who resists their attempts to take his property, violate Muslim women, and torture every Muslim they capture, male or female, until they disclose the whereabouts of some Muslim property. News of them reached the imam, and the ruler ordered some men to go and prevent them from killing Muslims and taking their property. They then rose against him, addressed those dispatched against them, and refused to obey the ruler. Is it lawful to fight them? If the ruler seizes some of their property and sells it to Muslims, is anyone permitted to buy it?

He answered:

Praise be to Allah. Yes, fighting these people is lawful; indeed, it is obligatory. If the ruler takes from their property an amount corresponding to what they took from the Muslims’ property, and does not know who is entitled to it, it is permissible to purchase it from him. If they have taken some of the Muslims’ property, the jurists differ concerning the seizure of their property. If the ruler follows either of the two opinions as his course of action, he is permitted to do so.

He—may Allah Most High have mercy on him—was asked:

Concerning those who spread corruption in the land and treat people’s property and lives as lawful to take, such as thieves and highway robbers: may a person give them some of his property, or may he fight them? If a man kills one of them, is he to be accused of hypocrisy? Does he incur any sin by killing someone who sought to kill him?

He answered:

The Muslims unanimously agree that fighting highway robbers is permissible. It is authentically established that the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, said:

“Whoever is killed defending his property is a martyr.”

If “highway robbers” demand the property of someone entitled to legal protection, he is not obliged to give them anything, by agreement of the imams. Rather, he should repel them using the least force necessary, escalating only as needed. If they cannot be repelled except by fighting, he may fight them. If he is killed, he is a martyr; if he kills one of them in these circumstances, no liability is incurred for that person’s death. Likewise, if they seek to kill him, he may repel them, even by killing them, according to unanimous agreement.

However, defending one’s property is not obligatory: a person may give them the property rather than fight them. As for defending one’s life, there are two opinions concerning whether it is obligatory, corresponding to two positions reported from Ahmad.

He—may Allah Most High have mercy on him—was asked:

Concerning a merchant whom a group of people defrauded, taking a sum of money from him. He brought them before the ruler, who punished them until they confessed to taking the money. They remain imprisoned over the money, but have given him nothing and insist that they will give him nothing.

He answered:

Praise be to Allah. Whoever among them has the money in his possession and refuses to hand it over is to be beaten until he hands over the money belonging to someone else that he holds. Whoever has concealed the money and denies knowing its whereabouts is to be beaten until he reveals its location.

As for anyone suspected of holding the money, when it is not known whether he has any of it, he may be beaten as punishment for the lying and wrongdoing he has committed. He is also to be pressed to disclose where the money is and required to produce it. And Allah knows best.
